Associate of Applied Science in Electrical/Electronic Engineering Technology from Zane State College.

## Academic Focus

The program provided a practical foundation in electrical and electronic engineering technology, combining circuit analysis, electronics, instrumentation, controls, automation, mathematics, physics, and related technical subjects.

The curriculum emphasized applying engineering concepts to physical systems through laboratory experimentation, measurement, troubleshooting, and hands-on implementation.

## Electrical and Electronic Systems

Coursework developed progressively from fundamental circuit analysis into more advanced electrical and electronic systems.

Laboratory work provided experience constructing and evaluating circuits, making electrical measurements, analyzing system behavior, and troubleshooting hardware when measured results differed from expected operation.

## Controls and Automation

The program included coursework in industrial controls, programmable logic controllers, and related automation technologies.

This provided practical experience connecting electrical and electronic systems with control logic and industrial equipment, establishing an early foundation for later work in automated test equipment and systems integration.

## Instrumentation and Troubleshooting

Laboratory activities emphasized the use of engineering test equipment to measure electrical behavior and diagnose system problems.

Students were expected to interpret measurements, identify discrepancies, determine likely causes, and apply appropriate corrective actions.

This practical troubleshooting experience became an important foundation for later professional engineering work involving complex electrical and automated systems.

## Mathematics, Physics, and Technical Foundations

Supporting mathematics and physics coursework provided the analytical foundation necessary for electrical and electronic engineering technology.

The combination of theory, laboratory work, and applied technical coursework emphasized understanding both the mathematical behavior of a system and its behavior when implemented in physical hardware.

## Engineering Development

The A.A.S. expanded upon the electro-mechanical foundation established through the earlier Associate of Technical Study and provided a more concentrated electrical and electronic engineering technology education.

It also formed part of the academic progression leading to the later B.S. in Engineering Technology and graduate study in Electrical Engineering.
