Associate of Technical Study in Electro-Mechanical Engineering Technology from Zane State College.

## Academic Focus

The program established an early foundation in electro-mechanical engineering technology, combining electrical, electronic, mechanical, mathematical, physical science, and engineering technology coursework.

The curriculum provided practical exposure to systems in which electrical and mechanical components interact, establishing the multidisciplinary foundation used throughout subsequent engineering education and professional work.

## Electrical and Electronic Systems

Coursework included fundamental electrical and electronic concepts, circuit analysis, measurement, and related engineering technology subjects.

Laboratory-based instruction provided experience applying theoretical concepts to physical electrical systems, using engineering instruments to measure system behavior and troubleshoot circuits.

## Mechanical and Electro-Mechanical Systems

The program also incorporated mechanical and electro-mechanical subjects, providing experience with the mechanical elements that interact with electrical and control systems.

This combination provided an early systems-level perspective on integrated equipment rather than focusing exclusively on a single engineering discipline.

## Engineering Mathematics and Science

Supporting mathematics and science coursework provided the analytical foundation necessary for subsequent engineering technology and engineering education.

The combination of technical theory, laboratory experience, mathematics, and physical science established the foundation for later study at the bachelor's and graduate levels.

## Engineering Development

This credential represents the beginning of my formal engineering technology education and established the multidisciplinary technical foundation that later developed through the A.A.S. in Electrical/Electronic Engineering Technology, B.S. in Engineering Technology, and graduate engineering education.
