I worked at The Ohio State University as a Student Tech Support Specialist, providing technical support within a large university computing environment.

## Technical Support

My work involved assisting users with computer hardware, software, and related technical issues.

The position required systematic troubleshooting: understanding the reported problem, gathering relevant information, isolating potential causes, implementing or recommending corrective actions, and confirming that the issue had been resolved.

## User Support

Technical problems had to be communicated to users with widely varying levels of technical experience.

This required translating technical information into clear instructions while gathering enough information from the user to accurately diagnose the underlying issue.

The experience reinforced the importance of communication as part of technical problem solving, particularly when the person experiencing a problem does not share the same technical background as the person troubleshooting it.

## Troubleshooting

Supporting computing systems provided early professional experience with structured troubleshooting and root-cause analysis.

Rather than treating symptoms independently, effective support required determining whether problems originated from hardware, software, configuration, connectivity, user interaction, or another part of the system.

## Professional Foundation

The position provided early experience working in a professional technical-support environment and helped develop skills in troubleshooting, technical communication, customer support, and systematic problem solving.

These skills later became directly applicable to engineering work involving increasingly complex electrical, software, and integrated systems.
