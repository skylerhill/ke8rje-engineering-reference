Served as Assistant Professor and Program Director for engineering technology programs, combining classroom and laboratory instruction with curriculum leadership, laboratory development, program assessment, accreditation activities, budgeting, industry collaboration, and student mentoring.

## Program Leadership

As Program Director, I was responsible for coordinating academic and technical activities across engineering technology programs.

Responsibilities included curriculum planning, course scheduling and coordination, laboratory operations, equipment planning, budgeting, program assessment, continuous improvement, and collaboration with faculty, administration, students, and industry stakeholders.

The role required balancing academic requirements with the practical needs of applied engineering education, including laboratory capability, equipment lifecycle, emerging technologies, and workforce expectations.

## Engineering Technology Instruction

I continued teaching electrical and electronic engineering technology coursework and laboratories while serving in the program leadership role.

Instruction emphasized the application of engineering principles through laboratory experimentation, measurement, troubleshooting, industrial equipment, automation, controls, and engineering design.

Maintaining direct involvement in classroom and laboratory instruction also provided feedback for curriculum planning and identification of opportunities for program and laboratory improvement.

## Curriculum Development

I evaluated and developed engineering technology curriculum to maintain alignment between program outcomes, technical content, laboratory experiences, and industry needs.

This work included review of course sequencing and learning objectives, development and revision of instructional material, coordination of laboratory activities, and evaluation of opportunities to introduce or expand technical subject areas.

Curriculum decisions considered both theoretical foundations and the practical engineering competencies students would need when entering industry.

## Laboratory Modernization

I led and supported modernization of engineering technology laboratory resources, including evaluation of existing equipment, identification of instructional needs, comparison of potential replacement technologies, equipment selection, laboratory planning, and implementation.

A representative effort involved modernization of robotics curriculum and laboratory resources, requiring consideration of industrial automation technology, instructional capability, safety, maintainability, available laboratory space, and long-term usefulness of the equipment.

## Capstone Design

I supported engineering technology capstone design activities in which students applied knowledge from across their programs to multidisciplinary engineering projects.

My responsibilities included technical mentoring, project planning, design review, troubleshooting, documentation, fabrication and testing support, and evaluation of completed engineering work.

Capstone projects provided an opportunity to reinforce the complete engineering process from problem definition and conceptual development through implementation, testing, documentation, and technical communication.

## Accreditation and Continuous Improvement

I contributed to accreditation activities associated with ABET Engineering Technology Accreditation Commission criteria.

This work included evaluation and documentation of curriculum, student outcomes, assessment processes, laboratory resources, and continuous-improvement activities.

Program assessment was used not simply as a reporting activity, but as an input to decisions regarding curriculum, laboratory resources, instructional methods, and other program improvements.

## Industry and Student Engagement

I worked with industry stakeholders to better understand workforce needs and maintain the practical relevance of engineering technology education.

I also mentored students in technical coursework, engineering projects, career development, and application of engineering principles to real-world problems.

## Technical Leadership

The position combined engineering education with technical and organizational leadership. Decisions involving curriculum, laboratories, equipment, projects, and program resources required consideration of technical performance, educational value, cost, safety, maintainability, and long-term program needs.

This role represented the culmination of my progression at Zane State College from adjunct faculty to full-time Instructor and ultimately Assistant Professor and Program Director.
