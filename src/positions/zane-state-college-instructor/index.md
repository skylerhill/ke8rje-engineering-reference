Served as a full-time instructor in Electrical and Electronic Engineering Technology, providing classroom and laboratory instruction across electrical, electronics, controls, automation, and related engineering technology subjects.

## Engineering Technology Instruction

I taught technical coursework spanning fundamental electrical theory through applied industrial systems.

Instruction included DC and AC circuits, electronics, sensors and instrumentation, electrical machines and transformers, industrial controls, programmable logic controllers, robotics, and related engineering technology subjects.

Courses combined analytical engineering concepts with practical application, emphasizing measurement, troubleshooting, equipment operation, and interpretation of real-world system behavior.

## Laboratory Instruction

Laboratory instruction was a major component of the position. I developed and delivered hands-on activities involving electrical circuits, instrumentation, sensors, motors and transformers, industrial controls, PLCs, robotics, and electromechanical systems.

Students used engineering test equipment and industrial hardware to construct, measure, troubleshoot, and evaluate systems rather than studying the concepts exclusively through simulation or lecture.

## Curriculum Development

My responsibilities expanded beyond delivery of existing coursework to include development and improvement of courses, laboratory exercises, instructional materials, and technical demonstrations.

Curriculum development required evaluating learning objectives, determining appropriate engineering theory and practical exercises, selecting laboratory equipment, and developing methods for assessing student understanding and technical performance.

## Hybrid and Virtual Engineering Education

During this period, I participated in restructuring engineering technology coursework for hybrid and virtual delivery.

This presented a particular challenge for laboratory-intensive engineering courses, where hands-on experimentation and equipment interaction are central to the learning process.

The work involved adapting instructional material and laboratory activities for alternative delivery formats while preserving the technical learning objectives and practical engineering content of the courses.

## Student Engineering Development

I worked closely with students on circuit analysis, laboratory experimentation, troubleshooting, controls, automation, and engineering problem solving.

Instruction emphasized systematic technical reasoning: understanding expected system behavior, making appropriate measurements, interpreting results, identifying discrepancies, and developing technically justified solutions.

This full-time Instructor role expanded the teaching and laboratory experience developed during my earlier adjunct appointment and established the foundation for my later responsibilities in program leadership, curriculum planning, laboratory modernization, and accreditation.
