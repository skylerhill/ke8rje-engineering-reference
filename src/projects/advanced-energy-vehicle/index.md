Multidisciplinary engineering project involving the development, integration, and testing of an autonomous electric vehicle with emphasis on energy efficiency, controls, system integration, iterative testing, and engineering design.

## Engineering Challenge

The project required development of a vehicle capable of completing defined operating objectives while minimizing energy consumption.

Performance depended on the interaction of multiple subsystems rather than any individual component. Mechanical characteristics, electrical hardware, propulsion, embedded control, vehicle behavior, and operating strategy all influenced the performance of the complete system.

The engineering challenge was therefore to develop and refine the vehicle as an integrated system.

## System Development

The vehicle was developed through an iterative engineering process involving design, implementation, testing, analysis, and refinement.

Subsystems had to operate together reliably while supporting the overall performance objectives of the vehicle.

Changes to one portion of the design could affect energy consumption or behavior elsewhere in the system, requiring design decisions to be evaluated in terms of their impact on overall vehicle performance.

## Electrical and Embedded Systems

Electrical and embedded hardware provided the interface between the vehicle's physical components and its control strategy.

The project required integration of electrical hardware, propulsion components, sensing and control functions, and embedded operation into a functioning vehicle.

This work provided experience considering electrical systems not as isolated circuits, but as interacting elements of a larger electromechanical system.

## Controls Engineering

Vehicle operation required development and refinement of control behavior appropriate to the required operating sequence.

Control decisions affected vehicle motion, repeatability, and energy consumption, making control strategy an important part of overall system performance.

Testing was used to evaluate actual vehicle behavior and refine the implementation when measured performance differed from expectations.

## Energy Efficiency

Energy consumption was treated as an engineering performance parameter rather than simply an outcome of completing the required task.

Design and operating decisions were evaluated in terms of their effect on vehicle efficiency, requiring tradeoffs between performance, control behavior, reliability, and energy use.

Measured results provided feedback for subsequent design iterations.

## Integration and Testing

System integration required the electrical, embedded, control, propulsion, and mechanical elements of the vehicle to operate together as a complete system.

Testing was performed iteratively throughout development rather than only after assembly was complete.

Observed vehicle behavior and measured performance were used to identify deficiencies, evaluate design changes, and improve subsequent iterations of the system.

## Engineering Project Management

The multidisciplinary nature of the project required coordination of technical work, design decisions, testing, and project activities across the development effort.

Engineering decisions had to account for technical requirements alongside available time, resources, integration dependencies, and overall project objectives.

## Engineering Experience

The Advanced Energy Vehicle project provided practical experience with systems integration, electrical engineering, embedded systems, controls, iterative testing, energy optimization, and multidisciplinary engineering design.

The project reinforced a systems-engineering principle that became increasingly important in my later work: the performance of an engineered system depends not only on the design of its individual components, but on the interactions among those components when the complete system is integrated and tested.
