Modernization of a legacy electrical test station used for precision drift testing. The project replaced aging and obsolete electrical hardware while preserving the highly sensitive control, excitation, and measurement behavior required by the existing test process.

## Engineering Challenge

The legacy station incorporated specialized circuitry and tightly controlled electrical signals that could not be replaced by simply selecting modern commercial equivalents. The behavior of the existing system first had to be understood at the circuit and system levels so that the replacement design could reproduce the required interfaces and operating characteristics.

The modernization therefore required reconstruction of the legacy design, development of replacement requirements, and integration of modern hardware with custom electrical circuitry where commercially available equipment could not directly provide the required behavior.

## Engineering Approach

Existing schematics, wiring, interfaces, operating sequences, and hardware were evaluated to establish the functional and electrical requirements of the test station.

The replacement architecture combined modern commercial components with purpose-designed electrical circuitry. Custom power-supply and control functions were developed where the application required electrical characteristics or control behavior that could not be adequately reproduced using standard equipment alone.

Particular attention was given to signal integrity, electrical stability, interface compatibility, and the interaction between power, control, instrumentation, and measurement functions because small deviations could affect the behavior of the overall test system.

## Electrical Design and Control

Electrical design activities included analysis of legacy circuitry, development of replacement schematics, component selection, power and control design, interface definition, and evaluation of the resulting system behavior.

The custom circuitry had to operate as part of the larger automated test system rather than as an isolated electronic assembly. Design decisions therefore considered both circuit-level performance and system-level interactions among the test station, instrumentation, control hardware, and equipment under test.

## Integration and Troubleshooting

The modernized system was integrated and evaluated incrementally. Measurements were used to compare actual electrical behavior against expected performance and to identify discrepancies between the reconstructed legacy behavior and the replacement implementation.

Troubleshooting required circuit-level analysis as well as systems-level evaluation of power, control, measurement, grounding, interfaces, and test sequencing.

## Verification and Validation

Verification and validation activities demonstrated that the replacement system reproduced the required functions of the legacy station and satisfied the defined electrical and functional requirements.

Testing included evaluation of power and control behavior, electrical interfaces, generated and measured signals, system operation, and repeatability. Engineering results and design documentation were maintained to support technical reviews and configuration-controlled implementation.

## Engineering Responsibilities

My responsibilities included legacy-system analysis, requirements development, electrical design, custom power and control circuitry, component evaluation and selection, interface definition, system integration, troubleshooting, verification and validation, and engineering documentation.

The project required applying circuit-level electrical engineering within a broader systems-engineering effort, with particular emphasis on preserving the behavior of a sensitive legacy test system while replacing obsolete hardware with a maintainable modern design.
