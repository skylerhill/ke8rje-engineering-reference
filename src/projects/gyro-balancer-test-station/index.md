Modernization of a legacy electrical test station used for precision gyro balancing operations. The project replaced an aging analog implementation with a modern digital automated test architecture while preserving the required behavior and interfaces of the legacy system.

## Engineering Challenge

The existing test station relied on obsolete hardware and legacy analog circuitry for measurement and control. Modernization required reconstructing the behavior of the existing equipment from available technical documentation and physical hardware, defining the required electrical interfaces, and developing a replacement capable of reproducing the necessary test functions with modern components.

A major engineering constraint was maintaining compatibility with the equipment under test while replacing portions of the original analog implementation with digitally controlled and measured hardware.

## Engineering Approach

The modernization effort began with reverse engineering and analysis of the legacy test station. Existing schematics, wiring, interfaces, operating sequences, and test requirements were evaluated to establish the functional and electrical behavior that the replacement system needed to reproduce.

System and electrical requirements were developed from this analysis and used to guide component selection, interface design, signal generation, measurement, control, and system integration.

The replacement architecture incorporated modern commercial hardware and digital data-acquisition and control capabilities in place of obsolete portions of the original analog system. Electrical interfaces were designed to provide the required excitation, measurement, control, and protection functions while maintaining compatibility with the existing test process.

## Integration and Troubleshooting

The redesigned electrical system was integrated incrementally and evaluated against expected test-station behavior. Troubleshooting included tracing signals through the system, comparing measured behavior with design expectations, evaluating interface conditions, and resolving discrepancies discovered during integration.

This work required coordination between requirements, electrical design, test equipment, instrumentation, software-controlled hardware, and the physical system being tested.

## Verification and Validation

Verification activities were performed to demonstrate that the modernized station satisfied its defined electrical and functional requirements. Testing included evaluation of generated and measured signals, interface behavior, system operation, and repeatability.

Engineering documentation and verification evidence were maintained under configuration control to support design reviews, system qualification, and continued operation of the equipment.

## Engineering Responsibilities

My responsibilities included legacy-system reconstruction, requirements development, electrical design, component evaluation and selection, interface definition, system integration, troubleshooting, verification and validation, and preparation of engineering documentation.

The project required systems-level consideration of how electrical hardware, instrumentation, automated test equipment, software-controlled interfaces, and the equipment under test interacted as a complete test system.
