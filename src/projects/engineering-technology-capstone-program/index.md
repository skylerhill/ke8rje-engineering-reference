Development and support of engineering technology capstone design activities in which students applied knowledge from across their programs to the planning, design, implementation, testing, and documentation of multidisciplinary engineering projects.

## Engineering Design Context

Capstone projects provided students with an opportunity to move beyond individual laboratory exercises and address larger engineering problems requiring integration of knowledge from multiple technical areas.

Projects required students to translate an identified problem or objective into a practical engineering solution while considering technical requirements, available resources, cost, schedule, fabrication, testing, and documentation.

The objective was to reinforce engineering as an iterative process rather than treating the final constructed system as the only measure of project success.

## Project Planning

Students were guided through development of project objectives, requirements, concepts, implementation plans, and schedules.

Early project activities emphasized understanding the problem before committing to a particular solution. Students were expected to evaluate alternatives, identify technical risks, plan their work, and justify significant design decisions.

This provided experience with engineering project management alongside the technical design work.

## Technical Mentoring

I provided technical guidance throughout project development while maintaining student ownership of the engineering solution.

Mentoring included design discussions, review of electrical and mechanical concepts, troubleshooting strategies, component and material considerations, fabrication approaches, measurement techniques, and evaluation of test results.

Rather than simply providing solutions when problems occurred, the process emphasized helping students identify likely causes, collect useful information, evaluate alternatives, and reach technically justified conclusions.

## Design Review

Projects were reviewed throughout development rather than only after completion.

Design reviews provided opportunities to evaluate requirements, proposed solutions, technical feasibility, interfaces, implementation progress, safety considerations, and unresolved engineering risks.

Students were expected to communicate their designs and respond to technical questions, reinforcing the role of design review and technical communication within the engineering process.

## Fabrication and Integration

Capstone projects frequently required construction and integration of electrical, mechanical, control, or automation elements.

I supported students as they translated designs into physical systems, addressing fabrication, assembly, wiring, interfaces, equipment operation, and integration issues encountered during implementation.

This stage reinforced the relationship between engineering documentation and the practical realities of building and integrating a working system.

## Testing and Troubleshooting

Students developed and performed testing to evaluate whether their completed systems satisfied project objectives and design requirements.

When results differed from expectations, I guided students through systematic troubleshooting and root-cause analysis using measurements, observations, engineering calculations, and understanding of system behavior.

Testing was treated as part of the design process rather than simply a final demonstration.

## Engineering Documentation

Students documented their engineering work throughout the project, including requirements, design decisions, analyses, drawings or schematics where appropriate, test results, and final conclusions.

Technical communication was incorporated into the capstone process so that students developed experience explaining not only what they built, but why particular engineering decisions were made and how the resulting system was evaluated.

## Program and Accreditation Context

Capstone activities provided an important opportunity to evaluate students' ability to integrate technical knowledge and apply engineering design skills developed throughout the curriculum.

Project outcomes and student work also contributed evidence relevant to program assessment and continuous-improvement activities associated with ABET Engineering Technology Accreditation Commission criteria.

## Engineering Responsibilities

My responsibilities included capstone curriculum development and coordination, project planning support, technical mentoring, design review, engineering project management, fabrication and integration support, troubleshooting, testing, documentation review, student evaluation, and coordination of resources required to support project execution.

The program provided students with experience across the complete engineering lifecycle: defining a problem, developing a design, building and integrating the solution, testing its performance, troubleshooting deficiencies, documenting the work, and communicating the results.
