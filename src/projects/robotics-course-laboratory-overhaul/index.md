Modernization of robotics curriculum and laboratory resources to improve the technical capability, relevance, and hands-on experience of the engineering technology program. The project combined curriculum development with evaluation, selection, planning, and implementation of industrial robotics equipment and supporting laboratory resources.

## Engineering Challenge

Robotics education requires laboratory equipment that can support both fundamental concepts and realistic industrial automation applications.

The modernization effort therefore required evaluating the capabilities of the existing curriculum and laboratory, identifying technical and instructional needs, and determining how new equipment and laboratory activities could support those needs within available program resources.

The project had to consider not only the technical capabilities of potential equipment, but also instructional usefulness, safety, maintainability, laboratory space, cost, and long-term value to the program.

## Needs Assessment and Benchmarking

I evaluated the existing robotics curriculum and laboratory resources to identify opportunities for improvement.

The process included reviewing instructional objectives, laboratory capabilities, available equipment, and the types of robotics and automation skills relevant to engineering technology students.

Potential approaches and equipment were compared based on their ability to support both introductory robotics concepts and more advanced industrial automation applications.

## Equipment Evaluation and Selection

Robotics hardware and supporting equipment were evaluated as integrated laboratory systems rather than as individual purchases.

Considerations included robot capabilities, controllers, programming environment, available interfaces, integration with other automation equipment, safety requirements, instructional flexibility, physical laboratory constraints, supportability, and cost.

The resulting equipment selections were intended to provide a platform that could support multiple laboratory activities and engineering concepts rather than a single narrowly defined exercise.

## Curriculum Development

The laboratory modernization was coordinated with development and revision of robotics instructional content.

Laboratory activities were designed to connect robotics theory with hands-on programming, system configuration, troubleshooting, automation, and interaction with physical equipment.

The objective was to give students experience working through the engineering process of understanding system behavior, developing a solution, implementing it on industrial hardware, testing the result, and troubleshooting unexpected behavior.

## Laboratory Planning and Implementation

Implementation required planning the physical and technical integration of robotics equipment into the laboratory environment.

This included consideration of equipment placement, student access, electrical and control interfaces, supporting hardware, safe operation, maintenance, and the ability to reconfigure equipment for different instructional activities.

The project therefore combined elements of robotics, industrial automation, laboratory development, engineering project management, and engineering education.

## Safety

Safety was incorporated into laboratory planning and instructional development because students would be working directly with powered industrial automation equipment.

Laboratory activities and equipment configurations were developed with consideration for controlled operation, appropriate student interaction, equipment limitations, and safe instructional practices.

## Program and Accreditation Context

The modernization supported broader continuous-improvement efforts within the engineering technology program.

Curriculum and laboratory resources were evaluated in the context of program learning objectives and ABET Engineering Technology Accreditation Commission criteria, helping connect investments in laboratory capability to measurable educational outcomes.

## Engineering Responsibilities

My responsibilities included needs assessment, technical benchmarking, curriculum development, equipment evaluation and selection, laboratory planning, implementation, safety considerations, budgeting and resource evaluation, and coordination with students, faculty, administration, vendors, and other stakeholders.

The project required balancing technical capability with educational value, safety, cost, maintainability, and long-term program needs.
