Restructuring of engineering technology coursework and laboratory activities for hybrid and virtual delivery while preserving technical learning objectives, practical engineering experience, and meaningful student interaction with laboratory concepts.

## Engineering Education Challenge

Engineering technology courses depend heavily on hands-on experimentation, instrumentation, troubleshooting, and interaction with physical equipment.

Transitioning these courses to hybrid and virtual formats therefore presented a different challenge than converting primarily lecture-based coursework. The objective was not simply to move instructional material online, but to determine how the underlying engineering learning objectives could still be achieved when students had reduced or modified access to traditional laboratory environments.

## Course Analysis

The effort began by evaluating course content and laboratory activities in terms of the technical skills and learning outcomes each activity was intended to develop.

Lecture material, demonstrations, laboratory exercises, assignments, and assessments were reviewed to determine which elements could transition directly to alternative delivery formats and which required redesign.

Particular attention was given to laboratory activities involving measurement, circuit behavior, equipment operation, troubleshooting, and interpretation of experimental results.

## Curriculum Restructuring

Course material was reorganized to support a combination of synchronous, asynchronous, hybrid, and virtual learning activities while maintaining continuity between engineering theory and application.

Instructional content was adapted so that students could develop the analytical understanding needed before applying those concepts during available laboratory activities.

The restructuring emphasized preservation of technical depth rather than simply reducing laboratory-intensive material because it was more difficult to deliver remotely.

## Laboratory Virtualization

Laboratory activities were evaluated individually to determine how their educational objectives could be preserved under alternative delivery constraints.

Where traditional laboratory interaction was limited, activities were restructured around engineering analysis, interpretation of measurements and system behavior, demonstrations, guided experimentation, and other methods appropriate to the technical objective.

Hands-on laboratory experiences were retained where they were necessary to develop skills that could not be adequately reproduced through virtual instruction alone.

## Assessment and Iteration

The effectiveness of revised instructional approaches was evaluated through student work, assessments, laboratory performance, and interaction with course material.

Course organization and activities were adjusted as necessary to improve clarity, technical effectiveness, and alignment with the original learning objectives.

This iterative process treated course redesign similarly to an engineering problem: establish requirements, develop an implementation, evaluate its performance, identify deficiencies, and refine the solution.

## Engineering Responsibilities

My responsibilities included analysis of existing course and laboratory requirements, curriculum restructuring, development and adaptation of instructional material, redesign of laboratory activities, development of alternative instructional approaches, student support, and evaluation of the resulting course delivery.

The project demonstrated the importance of separating the **learning objective** from the traditional method used to achieve it, allowing engineering technology education to adapt to significant delivery constraints without abandoning the practical and analytical skills the coursework was intended to develop.
